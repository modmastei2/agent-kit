# Example: Civic Building (Town Hall)

Demonstrates deriving grammar from a civic structure and reapplying it elsewhere without
copying — see references/taste-analysis.md, "Extracting design grammar instead of
copying."

## Intent
- Type: town hall
- Style: classical civic, adapted (user-specified: "grand but not gothic")
- Purpose: seat of local governance, must read as the settlement's most important
  building
- Scale: large relative to surrounding houses

## Design thesis
A symmetric, axial building organized around a projected central portico, with a single
low dome marking the council chamber behind it — order and hierarchy expressed through
composition, not surface ornament.

## LOD1 — Composition
- Primary mass: wide, low, horizontally organized main block — deliberately horizontal to
  read as institutional/grounded rather than a tower-driven landmark.
- Secondary mass: central portico projects forward from the main block's façade plane,
  immediately breaking the "flat façade" failure mode along the most important elevation.
- Silhouette: one low dome centered over the portico is the sole vertical accent —
  restrained per the style brief, so it isn't competing with the width of the primary
  mass.
- Symmetry: full bilateral symmetry — appropriate here because civic authority is exactly
  the case symmetry is suited for (references/composition.md); this does not mean
  symmetry becomes the default for every future structure.
- Palette: quartz/smooth-stone primary wall, dark stone banding as accent at floor lines,
  minimal material count to keep the civic reading clean.

## Design grammar extracted (for reuse, not for copying this building elsewhere)
```
wide low primary mass
+ forward-projected central portico
+ single restrained dome accent
+ symmetric façade with floor-line banding
```

## Reapplying the grammar without copying
- A blacksmith derived from this grammar keeps the "one projected element breaks the
  façade" idea but as a forge bay, not a portico, and drops the dome and symmetry
  entirely — a blacksmith isn't a civic landmark.
- A library derived from this grammar keeps the low dome as a reading-room skylight
  motif and the floor-line banding, but replaces full symmetry with a single
  asymmetric wing for stacks, since the brief for that building calls for more informal
  character.

Neither result would be mistaken for a small town hall — the grammar transferred, the
building didn't.
